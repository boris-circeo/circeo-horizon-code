## Notes, reductions, anomalies

* Runtime (single 2-core machine, 2 workers): Oracle_REF_H 1.20 h, B1_5d_REF 1.09 h, B1c_5d_REF 1.11 h each, ENS_REF s1 6.14 h (about 31 min per month), Experiment B about 1.5 h. Total about 12.7 CPU-hours.
* Reduction: ENS_REF seed 2 was DROPPED (projected total CPU above the 11 h budget); ENS members stayed at 20. Forecast-error sensitivity is therefore measured on B1c_5d_REF (seeds 1, 2) only. Server restarts killed the runs twice; all runs resumed from their monthly checkpoints.
* Integration check (5 d from 2015-01-15, v2 vs `ForecastErrorParams.legacy()`): B1c_5d_REF 255.4 vs 260.6 MWh, ENS_REF 257.5 vs 264.2 MWh (latency costs about 2 %), 0 violations, 0 guard interventions; no policy fix needed (DP/refiner/ensemble use absolute times and plan from the current time/position).
* Single-seed monthly regrets are noisy: B1c seeds 1 and 2 differ by up to 143 MWh in a month (Feb: CF 0.799 vs 0.870, Jul 0.671 vs 0.607) although annual totals agree within 0.3 %. Monthly rankings among causal policies are therefore not significant; the annual ENS_REF edge (+1.2 %, 9/12 months, t = 1.0) is within seed noise.
* Regrets of a persistent ship (8-12 % annual) are larger than the 30-day S3 missions (3-6 %): latency in the forecast model, the weak-wind season (Jun-Sep CF 0.67-0.80 even for hindsight), and the approximate nature of Oracle_REF_H (15 d look-ahead).
* The climatological terminal value uses the annual 2015 climatology (same-year leakage, as the B0 point).
* B0 `min margin` reflects a hold point far from the coast, not a tight track. Coast violations: 0 for every run; guard interventions: 0.
* Lorient `t(prod)` is a noisy indicator (threshold = 90 % of the policy's own same-window mean power in A); `t(50 km)` is the robust one (median about 4-5 h).
* ENS_REF s1 export in the inspector: `outputs/inspector_S4.html` (6.8 MB), data `outputs/s4_inspector/year.json.gz` (4.9 MB); earlier synthetic smoke export moved to `outputs/s4_inspector_old/`.
