# S5 sensitivity campaign — results (WP4)

Campaign `scripts/s5/campaign.yaml`: 398 cases, run 2026-11-11 → 2026-11-12, code 54aadcb, data
23ccd01 (ERA5 2015 fingerprint d18ed34e…, BETA polar 283f2dc5…), all recorded in MLflow
(`docs/TRACKING.md`). Full table: `docs/s5/sensitivity_table.md` (generated by
`scripts/s5/sensitivity.py`).

**Protocol.** Persistent ship (no port calls) on the six bench windows of 2015 (1 Jan, Mar, May,
Jul, Sep, Nov; 30 days each), started at the annual best offshore point, 40 km / 3 h grid, hourly
ERA5 truth, manoeuvre losses on, synthetic forecasts derived from ERA5 (exchangeable perturbation
ensemble, 6 h latency for ENS, 4 h control). Every variant changes ONE knob of the default
`B1c_5d_REF` router (5 d deterministic DP on the control forecast, climatological terminal value,
48 h continuous refiner, replan 6 h) or of the environment. Effects are **paired** differences
on the same windows and forecast seed: `delta ± se`, with `z = delta / se`. We read |z| ≥ 2 as an
effect, |z| < 1 as noise. Noise floor: two forecast seeds of the default router give 79.9 and
80.7 % (0.8 pt apart over 6 windows); window-to-window CF spans 70–92 %.

References (mean CF over the 6 windows): Oracle_REF_H (15 d hindsight + refiner) **86.2 %**,
B1c **79.9–80.7 %**, ENS_REF 80.2 %, B1m (ensemble mean) 79.7 %, fixed optimal point B0 **58.8 %**,
greedy forecast-following rules 33–34 %.

## 2. What it means

**The forecast is the bottleneck, not the optimiser.** With a perfect forecast the operational
router reaches 85.0 %, 1.1 pt from the hindsight oracle; each additional "nominal error" costs
7–8 pt. Every architecture knob together (refiner, terminal value, replan) moves the result by
less than the forecast-error level alone.

**The kind of error matters as much as its size.** At equal error variance, errors that only
displace/delay the weather systems cost little (81.3 %), errors on wind *intensity* plus local
noise cost a lot (69.2 %). The ship can wait or reposition for a mistimed system, but it cannot
recover from planning on wind that is not there. This is the main sensitivity to the quality of
*our simulation of forecasts from ERA5*: the intensity/displacement split of the synthetic error
must be calibrated on real archived forecasts (GEFS reforecast / ECMWF ENS) before absolute CF
figures are quoted. Today's default mix behaves like the "analog" model (real ERA5 differences
reused as errors): 79.4 vs 79.9 %.

**The ensemble does not pay at nominal error.** ENS (20 members, 6 scenarios, two-stage) equals
B1c (80.2 vs 80.3 %) and costs 5× the CPU; it gains +1.9 pt only under intensity-type errors and
+1.2 pt with displacement errors. None of its knobs (members, scenarios, CVaR, spread) moves the
result beyond noise on 4 windows. The ensemble remains the right tool *if* real forecasts turn out
intensity-dominated — to be decided with real data in S5.x.

**Polar error is a production risk, not a routing risk.** A ±10 % error on the truth polar moves
CF by ±8 pt but leaves the router's regret unchanged: decisions are robust to a scale error.
An angular error (±10° on TWA) costs 2–3.5 pt, also for the oracle, so it is again mostly lost
production rather than wrong routing.

**Simple architecture suffices.** Horizon beyond 3 d, finer grid, longer arcs, longer refiner
windows, manoeuvre modelling, storm penalties, coast margins up to 40 km: all within noise.
What must stay: a continuous refiner (+2.9 pt), a terminal value when the horizon is short
(+2.6 pt; a 10 d horizon makes it useless, so it is a cheap substitute for horizon), replanning at
6 h or faster (24 h: −1.4 pt).

**Heuristics are not an option.** Forecast-following greedy rules (go to the best forecast hold
cell within 24 h, or chase the strongest wind) reach 33–34 %, far below the fixed point (58.8 %):
the ship cannot chase systems (patterns move 2.4× faster than the ship, see
`weather_analysis.md`), it must be placed ahead of them.

## 3. Caveats

* One year (2015) and six 30-day windows; paired SEs are 0.3–1 pt, so effects below ~1 pt are not
  resolved. Multi-year ERA5 (S5.x) is needed for regime dependence (NAO+/−).
* Forecast errors are synthetic. All forecast conclusions are conditional on the error model,
  which is why the structure scan was run.
* The v1 refiner had a 20 s IPOPT CPU-time limit (results depend on machine load) and the
  terminal climatology included the test window (leak). Both are fixed in HORIZON v2 and
  quantified by the v2 validation campaign (`scripts/s5/campaign_v2.yaml`, `docs/s5/v2_validation.md`).
* The domain stops at 32°W while the best climatological cell sits on that edge; a mid-Atlantic
  start gains 1.9 pt. Extending the ERA5 box west (≥ 45–50°W) is an S5.x data item.
